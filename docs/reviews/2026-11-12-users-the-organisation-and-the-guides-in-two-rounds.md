## Question

The product owner asked for a full and careful review of the whole flow: how users are handled, how the organisation
works, and everything around them. The guides were to be read closely. The question was whether everything is
perfect. Every finding was then to go through a second round that checks it.

This is the tenth review of the area. The nine before it are listed under Method. It was asked for after
[D114](../decisions/D114-person-profiles-show-employments-and-categorised-phone-numbers.md) had added employments and
phone numbers to the profile, and the guides had been handed to the team behind Sundsvall's directory.

## Assessment

**No, it is not perfect.** 101 findings stand: 94 after two rounds and merging, and seven (X1 to X7) in the two
commits that landed while the review ran. Three are high, 24 medium, 56 low and 18 nits. The first of those commits,
`bb2abf4`, fixed three of them and part of four more. Most are small.
The three high ones are not. Five first sign-ins at once can stop Clew for everybody (L1). An audience made of
provisioned groups admits nobody (A2). A remote connector is sent each person's groups and capabilities, beyond the
published actor (A1).

**What the earlier reviews established still holds.** The reviewers checked it again:

- A person's grant is derived exactly: it equals a fresh derivation from the person's memberships, and every change to
  it writes an audit row. A deactivation takes effect on the next request, in background work and before every delegated
  use of a token.
- Every SCIM operation is scoped to the organisation of its provisioner key. A key is looked up by its digest. A
  revoked key, and a replaced key past its grace period, answer 401.
- Every write commits with its audit row in one transaction. Locks are taken in one order, though a change of
  `externalId` can still deadlock with a group write that adds the same person (S4).
- The memory and PostgreSQL adapters agree on everything the reviewers compared, apart from one rule for group keys
  (B2), a database created with the C locale (L13), and a NUL character in an id, a key or a search, which only
  PostgreSQL refuses (S10, K3).
- The tree's rules hold through a large organisation's year: building, moving, renaming, re-keying, merging,
  splitting and deleting units.
- The example provisioner works in the right order and never leaves a person on no unit during a move. A second run
  changes nothing. It pushed the example directory of 250 people and 48 units through the real routes, and the check
  read the result as the guide says.
- The guides' printed outputs, walkthrough and sign-in table match the code, and so does the refusal table but for its
  row on an empty required attribute (P5). Every link resolves.
- Requests shaped as Microsoft Entra ID and Okta send them are read correctly.

**What is wrong falls into a few patterns.**

- *The actor is cut at the wrong layer.* An audience check gets the six-field actor, which has no groups, so an
  audience of provisioned groups admits nobody (A2). A remote connector gets the whole context,
  groups and capabilities included, beyond the published actor (A1). The two fixes must land together.
- *The database pool can stall.* Five first sign-ins at once can deadlock the five-connection pool for good. The
  binding reads through a second connection inside its own transaction (L1). A token refresh holds a
  connection while it waits for the identity provider, and nothing checks that the database still answers.
- *A sign-in binding cannot be repaired.* A changed source id, a deleted and recreated account, a new app
  registration and a swap of account names all end in a refusal. Only a hand edit of `auth_identity_binding` repairs
  it, and some of these refusals do not record whom they refused (L2, L6 to L8).
- *A user name is a key where it should not be.* Cards, started actions, erasure and the Nextcloud
  connector find a person by user name, so a rename loses records and a reused name gives them to the newcomer, and
  the guides do not warn of it (N1, N2, N4). The talks planner finds a participant by work address in the same way
  (N3).
- *Removals wait on unrelated data or on the daily run.* One bad organisation row in the export
  stops the example's removals: leavers stay active, or people keep the manager roles, capabilities and placements the
  source withdrew (E1). The change tables have no row for a plain group, so a person taken out of a group mapped to a
  capability keeps it until the daily run (P1). Of two leavers who held one account name in turn, the first is refused
  on every run, with nothing in the source to correct (E2).
- *A request is still read differently by path.* A group `PUT` without `externalId` drops a unit's key, while a user
  `PUT` keeps it (S2). Since `bb2abf4` an operation without a value is refused on a user, while on a group it still
  takes a unit's key or parent away (X1).
- *The guides miss what the identity-provider and hosting teams will meet.* Entra ID offers no back-channel logout
  (L5). Every sign-in forces credentials (L9). "Close the account" lets the identity provider delete an account that a
  returner needs (L7). The hosting section leaves out the database, the master key and the migrations (P2).
- *A person with two managers has no rule.* A talk that one manager booked is missing for the other, who is asked to
  book it again, and either manager can close an action point the other owns (M1). A former manager's own points leave
  Hem while their cards stay (M2).
- *Personal data reaches logs and traces.* A failed statement logs its bound values (T1), and a span's `url.full`
  names the person (T2).
- *Decisions reached the code before the records, references, figures and tests that describe them* (O1 to O5, O7,
  W2). Two records are marked accepted though nothing outside the repository has tested them (O6).

**What landed while the review ran left gaps.** `bb2abf4` closed what the profile review asked for on users. On a group,
an operation without a value still takes a unit's key or parent away and answers 200 (X1). On a user, a path-less one
still refuses the deactivation beside it (X2). An operation on `title` without a value now refuses the whole request,
where D110 says a title never refuses one (X3). The
[note on work placements](2026-11-12-municipal-employment-and-work-placements.md) in `0126fa9` gives "placement" a
second meaning beside that of
[D99](../decisions/D99-units-form-a-tree-pushed-over-scim-and-a-manager-works-with-their-direct-reports.md) (X4), and
the questions it leaves open are in no row of the [open questions](../maintainers/open-questions.md) (X5).

### L. Sign-in, sessions and the binding to a person

| # | Finding | Severity | Evidence | On `0126fa9` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | **Five first sign-ins at the same moment can lock all five database connections for good, and from then on every request of every person hangs until the server is restarted, while the health checks stay green.** `bindFirstLogin` holds one of the pool's five connections (`packages/db/src/index.ts:11`) in a transaction and calls `readAccess` inside it (`packages/db/src/auth.ts:96, 120`), which needs a second connection (`packages/db/src/auth.ts:47-54`); a bound, enabled person without a grant makes the first of these calls at every sign-in. When five such transactions hold all five connections nothing ends the wait: the pool has no queue or query timeout, PostgreSQL's statement and idle-in-transaction timeouts are off, and `/api/health` and `/api/ready` never touch the database. On a quiet pool the five must start within the time one binding takes, about 2 ms locally, but a token refresh holds a connection while it waits for the identity provider, and with slow refreshes alongside, five first sign-ins a second locked the pool within 1 to 7 seconds. | L2 | **When an account name changes hands and the identity provider has the change before Clew, a person who signs in for the first time in between is bound for good to the other holder's row, and that holder is then refused.** A first sign-in binds the subject to whoever holds the claimed name in Clew at that moment, if that person is enabled and has never signed in under this issuer (`packages/db/src/auth.ts:81-120`). ## Found in passing, outside the area

**A deferred attention delivery is never released on PostgreSQL.** This was read, not run, and lies outside the users
and organisation area:

- `admitDelivery` gives an automatic delivery a later `dueAt` when it arrives in quiet hours, before 06:00 or from
  17:00, or over the day's budget (`apps/server/src/threads/attention-domain.ts:16-28`, D47). A reminder is never
  deferred. It stores the delivery as `waiting` and writes an `attention-release` row into the job table
  (`apps/server/src/threads/adapters/attention-postgres.ts:42-46`).
- It writes the row directly. It starts no workflow for it. `scheduleJob` enqueues a row's `scheduledJob` workflow in
  the same transaction (`packages/db/src/jobs.ts:12-24`, D86), and the execution projection does so for its own rows
  (`apps/server/src/composition/execution-projection.ts:192-199`). Until D86 (`36a11cb`, 2026-11-09), the outbox loop
  for scheduled jobs started a workflow for every such row. D86 retired the loop and left this insert as it was.
- `runScheduledJob` is the only caller of `attention.release` (`apps/server/src/composition/jobs.ts:6-15`), and only
  that workflow runs it. The polling scheduler in `apps/server/src/scheduler/usecases.ts:65` is never started, as D47
  intends, and could not release the row if it were: it claims only rows marked `due` (`packages/db/src/jobs.ts:43-48`),
  this row is stored as `temporal`, and its `run` refuses the kind `attention-release`
  (`apps/server/src/scheduler/usecases.ts:39`).
- `pending()` reads only `ready` deliveries.

So on PostgreSQL a deferred delivery stays `waiting` for good, and its toast "Nya uppgifter finns i Att göra." never
appears. The card itself is still listed, since deferred attention never hides work (D47). The test in
`tests/db/postgres-refresh-attention.test.ts` calls `release` directly and does not cover this path.

On 2026-11-12 the product owner asked for every finding to be fixed, large and small, in one pull request based on
`main`, and for every open decision to be taken by whoever fixed them, as they judged best, with the reasons written
down. Every finding is fixed, and each row above says so. The thirteen questions are answered, and their row in the
[open questions](../maintainers/open-questions.md) is gone, with the register's question on binding a person to a new
account, which Q8 settles. No fix needed new surface
([D101](../decisions/D101-the-first-real-integration-is-nextclouds-approval-app-and-new-surface-waits-for-it.md) item 4).

**The decisions.** Three are new records, `provisional` until something outside the repository has tested them
([D102](../decisions/D102-a-decision-is-provisional-until-something-outside-the-repository-has-tested-it.md)):

- [D115](../decisions/D115-a-sign-in-follows-the-identity-providers-session.md) (Q9, L5): a sign-in follows the identity provider's session, and a sign-out there ends Clew's. Under
  OIDC the identity provider owns authentication policy, and forcing credentials at every sign-in defeats single
  sign-on and trains people to type their password more often. An organisation that wants a fresh prompt, on shared
  computers for example, sets it in its identity provider. Entra ID's only sign-out signal is front-channel, so Clew
  also answers OpenID Connect Front-Channel Logout 1.0; without it, signing out at Microsoft would leave the Clew
  session open for up to eight hours.
- [D116](../decisions/D116-clew-keys-its-own-records-about-a-person-by-the-persons-id.md) (Q11): Clew keys its own records about a person by the person's id. The id never changes and is never
  reused, so a rename loses nothing and a reused name reaches nobody else's records. Moving the stored names at each
  rename would have fixed renames only, and only if every provisioner renamed a leaver first. The user name stays the
  key on the wire, and an approved action whose user name has come to name someone else is not sent.
- [D117](../decisions/D117-clew-empties-expired-sign-in-data-itself-and-a-leavers-retention-is-the-organisations-decision.md) (Q7, L3, P4, R3): Clew empties expired sign-in data itself, and how long a leaver's record is kept is
  the organisation's decision. Here the decision goes further than the recommendation, which was to leave retention as
  an open question. A municipality's records can be official documents that only its own retention rules may cull, so
  Clew cannot choose a period; but that decision means nothing without a way to carry it out, so an operator now
  erases a deactivated person's record with `scripts/person-erase.ts`, and a person pushed by mistake can be erased.
