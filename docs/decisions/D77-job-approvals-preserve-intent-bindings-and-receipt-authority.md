# D77 · Job approvals preserve watch intent, binding identity and receipt authority

**Status:** accepted, built 2026-11-07 · **Date:** 2026-11-07 · **Area:** integrations

**Decision.** The job API retains the first committed watch schedule on retries, checks the current binding behind every proposed step, and protects stored receipts with their producing authority.

1. A job assembled from relative watch windows supplies an internal schedule origin to the execution ledger. Its approval digest compares watch offsets and duration, while the saved job and watch intent retain absolute check times. A later or concurrent identical approval returns that first committed job and creates no extra audit or delivery rows. Changed execution inputs or effective watch policy still conflict. Internal callers that supply explicitly absolute schedules retain their existing digest contract.
2. Every assembled step carries its binding id; its opaque step id is derived from that binding and its position in the follower's response. Each source entry carries a binding revision fingerprint covering configuration and update time. Approval refreshes governance and verifies the current binding set, revisions, follower availability, audience, connector ownership and step association. The check runs again after individual step authorisation, immediately before the ledger transaction. Watch caps are keyed by binding, so two followers from one connector cannot overwrite each other's limits. Binding edits gate new approvals; they do not cancel work already committed. Dispatch still checks current source authority.
3. A job or execution snapshot requires the original person, unit and non-empty authorisation version. Job reads check every participating execute/watch capability and withhold the whole job if one is unavailable; they check person authority again before returning. Approval responses use that same read boundary after commit. Repeated approvals, reconciliation, compensation and calendar approval lookup cannot disclose or reuse another producing authority's evidence. Evidence stays stored for recovery and audit.
4. The binding revision is change-detection metadata, not a signature of the complete proposal. The API still accepts the proposal content from the client. A durable immutable reviewed proposal, creator-schema validation of facts, and result-driven continuation remain separate work.

**Compatibility.** This changes Clew's job-approval envelope, not the external connector's `StepDraft` or protocol version. Clients must echo `steps[].binding` and `sources[].bindingRevision` from assembly and treat step ids as opaque. Unapproved proposals assembled before this change must be assembled again. Existing approved jobs and their stored executions/watch intents are retained without rewriting identities, schedules or digests. Recover those jobs through their stored identifiers; do not use a new decision id to retry an uncertain approval. No database migration is required.

**Why.** The [task-continuation exploration](../reviews/2026-11-07-multi-connector-task-continuation.md) reproduced a retry conflict caused solely by elapsed time, approval through a disabled binding, and receipt disclosure after a unit/grant change. Binding-aware step identities also remove collisions and window-cap interference when one connector has several followers.

**Replaces.** Refines [D55](D55-a-job-is-assembled-from-what-connectors-follow-approved-once-and-run-per-system.md)'s initial job implementation and extends [D75](D75-source-snapshots-retain-the-authority-that-produced-them.md)'s snapshot rule to direct job/execution receipt paths. It does not introduce workflow chaining or change source-system ownership. D75's requirement for propagation of source-only entitlement changes into Clew grants or connector governance remains in force.
