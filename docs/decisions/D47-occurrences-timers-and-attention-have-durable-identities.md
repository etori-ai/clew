# D47 · Occurrences, timers and attention have durable identities

**Status:** accepted, implemented; amended by D85 (2026-11-09: the durable engine is DBOS Transact, so "Temporal" below reads "the durable engine"; recovery keys on stable executor ids and the application version, see D85) · **Date:** 2026-11-05 · **Area:** platform

**Decision.** PostgreSQL owns work occurrence identity, timer intent, follow-up bounds and attention admission. Threads project those records into cards. Temporal runs the waits and retries after committed intent; it cannot invent a human approval. [D45](D45-approved-intent-precedes-source-dispatch.md) also governs calendar create, move, cancel, confirmation and compensation.

- An occurrence is identified by person, connector, assignment, source key and recurring period. Revision changes can reopen work with an explanation. Local dismissal suppresses the unchanged occurrence; it never claims the source completed it. Only a complete, exhausted observation in the same scope, or an explicit delta tombstone, can resolve absence. Legacy topic-only scans remain conservative.
- Per-source refresh requests coalesce in PostgreSQL. Four workers use separate leases and ten-second source deadlines; reads return the persisted view. A projection version captured before source I/O prevents a late scan from overwriting an intervening action. Current authority is checked again after I/O.
- Reminder and snooze changes commit an absolute due time, generation, desired state, thread update and audit together. Workflow acknowledgement is separate. Delayed start, cancellation and replacement are fenced by generation, and duplicate firing cannot create a second delivery. Existing workflow functions remain replay compatible.
- Calendar approval persists a bounded follow-up policy: at most two reminders per invitation, two working days apart, expiring after 30 days. Its first check can be scheduled with approval, but it waits behind an unconfirmed execution and never sends an invitation itself. Each check re-evaluates current assignment, authorization, participant status and expiry. Reminder cards, counters, audit and the next timer commit together. Historical invitations without an approved policy are paused, not silently enrolled.
- The old `job` table remains a Temporal dispatch outbox with natural IDs. New jobs and queued legacy jobs have one Temporal owner. A legacy `running` job becomes `legacy-running` and requires an operator's drain/reconciliation evidence before adoption. The old polling runner is not started by the application.
- Attention admission uses a locked per-person Stockholm day and stable delivery IDs. Automatic interruptions use five-minute buckets and a maximum of three outside review/bundle windows; quiet hours defer to 06:00, and exhausted budgets defer to 12:00, 16:00 or the next review. The scheduled windows have a 15-minute grace period. User-requested reminders keep their chosen time and do not consume this automatic budget. Deferred attention never hides work.
- The shell observes persisted deliveries on every signed-in page. Claiming a batch is transactional across tabs; reconnects do not claim it again. A claim is an **at-most-once announcement**, not proof that a human saw it: a tab can die after claiming and before rendering. The card and reminder bell remain the durable recovery surface. SSE remains invalidation only, as in D46.

**Why.** A topic, JavaScript timeout or in-memory counter cannot establish occurrence identity, safe cancellation or cross-tab delivery. Keeping state and audit in PostgreSQL makes failures inspectable; Temporal supplies orchestration without becoming a second business authority. Calendar uses stable UIDs, conditional revisions and read-back; uncertain effects stay unknown and are never blindly replayed.

**Evidence and limits.** Memory/PostgreSQL contracts cover rollback, concurrency, bounded follow-up, occurrence completion and attention ownership. Node/PostgreSQL/Temporal tests cover the independent source's lost reply and restart, timer redelivery/cancellation/replacement and old-history replay. A synthetic upgrade and backup/restore rehearsal preserves evidence. Browser tests cover untouched-tab invalidation and two-tab claims. These do not prove every process-death boundary, a real-source pilot, a live 30-second delivery SLO or manual screen-reader conformance. See the [acceptance matrix](../reference/reliability-acceptance.md).

**Replaces.** Extends D17, D45 and D46; replaces the old Postgres-polling ownership described in the previous scheduled-work implementation. Preserves D33's query cache and no browser polling. Does not accept D19's proposed CloudEvents endpoint or choose a pilot source.
